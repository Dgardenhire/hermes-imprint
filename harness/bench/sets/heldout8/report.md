# Tallow Creek Respite Cooperative
## FY2026 Annual Program Report
### July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026 | Marrow Junction and the Upper Tallow townships

## Who we are

Tallow Creek Respite Cooperative gives unpaid family caregivers a few hours of their lives back. We train and employ local respite workers who go into the home and stay with an adult who needs supervision, most often a parent or spouse living with dementia, Parkinson's disease, or the after-effects of a stroke. While the worker is there, the caregiver sees a doctor, sleeps, or sits in a parked car and does nothing at all. We have done this since 2019 with a staff of two coordinators and a pool of part-time workers who live in the communities they serve.

Families pay on a sliding scale from zero to $14 an hour. In FY2026, 64 percent of families paid nothing.

## What changed this year

FY2026 was the first full year after we moved scheduling off paper and onto a shared calendar, hired a second coordinator, and raised respite worker pay from $16.50 to $19.00 an hour. The pay raise mattered most. In FY2025 we lost 11 of 23 workers during the year, and every departure put families back on the waitlist. In FY2026 we lost 4 of 31.

| Measure | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Caregiver families served | 118 | 171 |
| Respite hours delivered | 6,240 | 9,880 |
| Average hours per family per month | 4.4 | 4.8 |
| Active respite workers (year end) | 23 | 31 |
| Workers who left during the year | 11 | 4 |
| Average days from referral to first visit | 41 | 12 |
| Emergency requests filled within 48 hours | 58% (29 of 50) | 83% (62 of 75) |
| Families on waitlist at year end | 37 | 9 |

The waitlist number is the one we watch. A caregiver who calls us is usually calling late, after a fall, a hospital stay, or a night without sleep. Forty-one days was too long. Twelve days is still not where we want it, but it is a different service.

Emergency requests, usually a caregiver's own surgery or hospital stay, grew from 50 to 75, and we filled a far larger share.

## Caregiver strain: what we found and what we cannot claim

At intake and again at six months, we ask caregivers to complete the 12-item short form of the Zarit Burden Interview, a widely used questionnaire that scores caregiver strain from 0 to 48. Higher scores mean more strain.

Among the 96 caregivers who completed both an intake and a six-month survey in FY2026, the average score fell from 27.4 to 19.8, a drop of 7.6 points. Sixty-one of the 96 were in the high-strain band (17 or above) at intake; 34 of those 61 were below 17 at six months.

We want funders to read this result carefully. First, only 96 of our 171 families completed the six-month survey. Some families left the program because the person they cared for moved into a nursing home or died; others simply did not return the form. Caregivers who stayed and answered may have been the ones doing better, so the average likely overstates the change. Second, we have no comparison group. Strain often eases for reasons that have nothing to do with us: a diagnosis settles, a sibling steps in, a medication starts working. Third, our coordinators administer the survey, and caregivers may want to give a good answer to the person who sends them help. We believe respite reduces strain, and the direction of these numbers fits that belief, but this is not proof that our program caused the drop.

In FY2027 we will contract with an outside evaluator to run follow-up calls so that the survey is not given by the people who schedule the care, and we will track why families leave.

## FY2027 plan: overnight respite and the two eastern townships

Two gaps showed up again and again in our intake calls this year.

The first is nights. Caregivers of people with dementia often lose sleep to wandering and sundowning, and four-hour daytime visits do not touch that. Twenty-two families asked for overnight coverage in FY2026, and we could not offer it.

The second is geography. Families in Corliss and Harrow Flat townships, the far eastern end of the county, made up 9 percent of our referrals but only 4 percent of our respite hours, because a worker's round trip there can run 70 miles.

In FY2027 we will:

1. Pilot overnight respite for up to 40 families, offering two overnight shifts (9 p.m. to 7 a.m.) per family per month for six months.
2. Hire two respite workers who live in Corliss and Harrow Flat.
3. Train 14 current workers in dementia-specific care, required for overnight shifts.
4. Bring in an outside evaluator to run follow-up surveys and exit interviews.

| FY2027 expansion line | Cost |
|---|---|
| Two additional respite workers, eastern townships (2.0 FTE wages and benefits) | $96,000 |
| Overnight pilot shift pay (up to 40 families, 6 months) | $38,500 |
| Dementia care training for 14 workers | $12,800 |
| Added rural mileage | $9,200 |
| Independent evaluation | $8,500 |
| **Total expansion cost** | **$165,000** |

The Upper Tallow County Area Agency on Aging has committed $45,000 toward this expansion through an amendment to our service contract, to cover part of the eastern township workers. We are seeking the remaining $120,000 from philanthropic partners.

If the overnight pilot works, we will know it by three things: the share of pilot caregivers who report at least five nights of unbroken sleep a month, their six-month strain scores gathered by the outside evaluator, and whether the families keep using the service. We will report all three, including if they disappoint.
