# Bramble Creek Mobile Market
## FY2026 Annual Program Report (July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026)

Bramble Creek Mobile Market is a refrigerated market truck that sells fresh, locally grown produce at fixed weekly stops across the western half of Hollins County. We buy from small farms within 60 miles, sell at or below grocery prices, and double the value of SNAP benefits spent on fruits and vegetables. Our home base is a leased bay behind the old feed store in Harrow Fork, a town of about 2,400 people that has had no full-service grocery store since 2019. The nearest supermarket for most of our customers is a 35 to 50 minute drive each way.

We are a staff of four: an executive director, a market manager, a driver and market associate, and a half-time outreach coordinator. A volunteer board of seven meets monthly.

## What we did this year

The truck ran 9 regular stops on a fixed weekly schedule: church lots, two senior housing complexes, a volunteer fire station, a Head Start center, and three crossroads stores. We operated 46 weeks of the year, taking a short break in late December and two weeks in February for scheduled truck maintenance.

- Market days held: 414 (9 stops x 46 weeks)
- Unique households served: 1,182
- Total customer visits: 9,860
- Average visits per market day: 23.8
- Produce distributed: 128,400 pounds
- Total sales at the truck: $142,300
- Sales paid with SNAP, including Double Up matching dollars: $61,200 (43 percent of sales)
- Local farms supplying the truck: 23
- Paid to local farms for product: $118,700

Our customer base skews older and lower income. In the intake survey we ask once a year, 41 percent of households included someone 65 or older, and 52 percent reported using SNAP, WIC, or both. About one in five households told us the truck is their main source of fresh produce for most of the year.

One stop underperformed. The Gallatin Road crossroads store averaged only 9 visits per market day, and we moved that slot to the Pine Hill senior apartments in March, where attendance has averaged 31. We also lost about four market days in January to ice on the county roads.

## Produce prescription pilot

In partnership with the Harrow Fork Rural Health Clinic, we ran a six-month produce prescription pilot from October 2025 through March 2026. Clinic providers enrolled adult patients with type 2 diabetes and a recent A1C of 7.5 or higher. Each enrolled patient received $40 per month in vouchers redeemable only for fruits and vegetables at the truck, plus a short monthly check-in with the clinic's community health worker.

The clinic enrolled 93 patients. Of those, 74 completed the six months and had both a starting and an ending A1C test on record.

**Result:** Among the 74 patients who completed the pilot, average A1C fell from 8.9 to 8.3, a drop of 0.6 points. Voucher redemption was high: 88 percent of issued vouchers were used.

**Caveat:** This was a small pilot without a comparison group, so we cannot say how much of that change came from the produce, from the monthly check-ins, from changes in medication, or from other factors. The 19 patients who dropped out are not included in the result, and people who stayed may have been more motivated to begin with. The clinic and our board both consider this an encouraging early signal, not proof. A second round in FY2027 will add a comparison group of clinic patients who receive check-ins but no vouchers.

| Vehicle operations (fuel, maintenance, insurance) | $38,500 |

Product purchased from farms ($118,700) is a cost of goods that is recovered through sales at the truck and is reported separately from operating expenses in our audited statements.

## Funding mix

Total operating revenue for FY2026 was $331,500 from five sources. The year closed with a surplus of $7,500, which the board placed in an operating reserve.

| Source | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Canby State Food Access Fund (state grant, year 2 of 3) | $120,000 | 36% |
| Weller Family Foundation (general operating) | $85,000 | 26% |
| Hollins County Health District (service contract) | $60,000 | 18% |
| Earned revenue (market margin and stop fees) | $41,500 | 13% |
| Individual donors (241 gifts) | $25,000 | 7% |
| **Total** | **$331,500** | **100%** |

Our largest single dependency is the state grant, which ends in June 2027. The board's goal is to bring any one source below 30 percent of revenue by FY2028.

## FY2027 plan: an eastern route

The eastern half of Hollins County has no mobile market, no farmers market, and two dollar stores. County health district data and our own waitlist point to five towns where we could run weekly stops: Ostler, Cane Fork, Millbury, Tarrant Springs, and Delphine. Twenty-nine households from those towns already drive to our western stops.

We plan to add a second, smaller refrigerated truck and a second route beginning in January 2027, with the same model: fixed weekly stops, local produce, SNAP doubling. In its first full year we expect the eastern route to serve 450 to 550 additional households across 5 stops.

The first-year cost of the eastern route is $145,000:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Used refrigerated box truck, purchase and outfitting | $68,000 |
| Driver and market associate (1.0 FTE, wages and benefits) | $46,000 |
| Fuel, insurance, and maintenance for year one | $14,500 |
| Double Up incentive dollars for new stops | $12,000 |
| Outreach and signage in five new towns | $4,500 |
| **Total** | **$145,000** |

Hollins County Health District has committed $30,000 toward the route through an expanded service contract. We expect to raise $20,000 from individual donors in a spring campaign. That leaves $95,000 to be raised from a foundation partner.

The produce prescription pilot will continue on the western route in FY2027 with the clinic, funded within the existing incentive budget.
