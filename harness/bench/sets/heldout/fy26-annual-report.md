# Fernwood Tool Library: FY2026 Annual Report (Sample)

*Sample organization. Fernwood Tool Library, Alder Falls, and every person named here are fictional.*

Fiscal year July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026. Prepared by the staff for members, partners and the board.

## Who we are

Fernwood Tool Library lends tools the way a public library lends books. Members borrow drills, ladders, tile saws,
carpet cleaners, pressure washers, sewing machines and garden tools for a week at a time, and bring them back for the
next neighbor. We opened in a rented storefront at 418 Wren Street in Alder Falls in 2019. Membership runs on a
sliding scale from $0 to $60 a year; about one member in four pays nothing.

We have one full-time director, one part-time shop lead, and 46 volunteers. The storefront is open 22 hours a week:
Wednesday and Friday afternoons and Sunday.

## The year in numbers

- **Members.** 1,962 active members on June 30, 2026, up from 1,410 a year earlier.
- **Loans.** 9,840 tool loans this year, up from 7,215 in FY2025. The average loan lasted 6 days. Four percent of loans
  came back late, and we wrote off 11 tools as lost or broken past repair.
- **Inventory.** 2,350 tools on the shelves, 310 of them added this year. Most new tools were donated; we bought 64.
- **Repair cafe.** 14 repair cafes, where volunteers fix what people bring in. Neighbors brought 611 items and our
  volunteers fixed 438 of them: lamps, toasters, bike brakes, zippers and one 1962 radio.
- **Volunteers.** 46 volunteers gave 3,120 hours, about the same as a second full-time staff member.

## What members told us

In May we sent a survey to every member and 512 answered.

- 58% said that without the library they would have bought the tool new. Another 23% said they would have hired
  someone or skipped the job.
- Members who answered said the library saved them a median of $180 over the year. This is the members' own
  estimate, not ours.
- **The hours are the problem.** When we asked what keeps people from borrowing more, 47% named our hours, far ahead
  of anything else. The next answers were "the tool I wanted was out" (19%) and parking (11%). Many wrote that they
  work weekday afternoons and cannot reach us before we close at 6 p.m.
- One member wrote: "I've walked past at 6:15 more times than I can count. The lights are on and the door is locked."

## The waiting list

On June 30, 344 hold requests were waiting for a tool to come back. Most were for the tile saw, the carpet cleaner and
the pressure washer. Holds wait an average of 11 days. On the three days we are open, a returned tool often goes back
out within the hour; on the four days we are closed, it sits on the shelf.

## Where members live

Members come from every part of Alder Falls, but not evenly. The Millbrook neighborhood holds 22% of the city's
renters, and only 9% of our members live there. Millbrook residents who answered the survey were the most likely to
name our hours (61%), because most of them work shifts.

## Money

The FY2026 budget was $212,400. We ended the year with expenses equal to income and no debt.

**Where the money came from**

| Source | Amount |
|---|---|
| Two foundation grants | $72,000 |
| Membership fees | $61,300 |
| City of Alder Falls neighborhood grant | $50,000 |
| Donations and repair cafe tips | $19,600 |
| Kettle Creek Hardware Co-op sponsorship | $9,500 |
| **Total** | **$212,400** |

**Where it went**

| Use | Amount |
|---|---|
| Staff (one full-time director, one part-time shop lead) | $128,000 |
| Rent for the Wren Street storefront | $36,000 |
| Buying and repairing tools | $24,600 |
| Insurance | $9,800 |
| Everything else (software, supplies, utilities) | $14,000 |
| **Total** | **$212,400** |

Membership fees covered 29% of the budget. The city grant is set for one more year and then must be applied for again.
Our reserve is $31,000, about seven weeks of costs. The board's goal is three months.

## What it costs to lend a tool

Dividing the year's budget by the year's loans, each loan cost us about $21.60. A new tile saw costs a member about
$400; a weekend rental at a store costs about $65.

## Looking ahead

The demand is there. Members want to borrow more, and the waiting list shows tools are not the only limit: the door is.
In FY2027 we want to stay open evenings and Saturdays. That takes a second paid person, because volunteers cannot
cover every new shift and the shop must have a trained lead on hand when power tools go out.

Staff worked out the cost of a part-time coordinator at 25 hours a week, with the longer hours for utilities and
supplies: $46,500 a year more than this year. With it, the storefront would be open 34 hours a week instead of 22,
adding Tuesday and Thursday evenings until 8 p.m. and Saturday from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Staff estimate the longer hours
would bring about 2,500 more loans a year once members learn the new times. That is an estimate, and we will report
what really happens.

The FY2027 budget the board will take up is $258,900.
