# Steady Roof Home Repair
## FY2026 Annual Program Report
### July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026 | Quillan Ridge, Ostrander County

## Who we are

Steady Roof Home Repair fixes the small, dangerous problems that push older homeowners out of houses they have owned for decades. We work only in Quillan Ridge and the three unincorporated townships around it. Every client is 62 or older, owns and lives in the home, and earns at or below 60 percent of the county median income. We do not do remodels. We do the repairs that decide whether someone can keep living at home: a rotted back step, a furnace that will not light, a bathroom with no grab bar, a basement stair with one loose rail and no light.

We run with 2.5 full-time staff: an executive director, a crew lead who is a licensed carpenter, and a half-time intake coordinator. Volunteers add labor on simpler jobs, and licensed electricians, plumbers and HVAC technicians are subcontracted.

The 412 jobs break down roughly as follows: 131 safety installations (grab bars, stair rails, exterior lighting, non-slip treads), 96 heating, plumbing and electrical repairs, 88 exterior repairs (steps, porches, roof patches, gutters), 61 weatherization jobs (door sweeps, window sealing, attic insulation), and 36 accessibility modifications (ramps, doorway widening, raised toilets).

We served 84 homes against a goal of 80, and ended the year with the longest waitlist in our history. The second fact drives the plan at the end of this report.

## What happened this year

**Intake grew faster than we could build.** We took 141 new applications in FY2026, up from 96 the year before. Most of the increase followed county Area Agency on Aging referrals starting in October 2025. Of the 141, 23 did not qualify and 4 withdrew. The rest either received repairs or joined the waitlist.

**Winter emergencies set the schedule.** Between December and February, 14 households called with no heat or a frozen pipe. Moving each of them to the front of the line was right, and it is the main reason the average wait grew from 3.8 months in FY2025 to 5.5 months this year.

**We changed how we assess a home.** Starting in September 2025, the crew lead and intake coordinator now walk every home together with a 38-point safety checklist before quoting any work. This added about 90 minutes per home and turned up problems the homeowner had not mentioned in 61 of the 84 homes, most often missing smoke detectors, loose stair rails and unlit entryways.

**The truck nearly ended the season.** Our only work truck, a 2011 model, was out of service for 17 working days in March and April. We borrowed a volunteer's pickup to keep going, which is not a plan we can repeat.

## Results, and what they do and do not show

Falls are the reason most families call us, so falls are what we try to measure. Twelve months after repairs, the intake coordinator calls each homeowner and asks the same questions we asked at intake, including whether they fell in the past year.

For homes completed in FY2025 whose 12-month calls came due in FY2026, we reached 52 of 79 homeowners (66 percent). Of those 52, 19 said at intake that they had fallen at least once in the year before repairs. At the 12-month call, 7 said they had fallen in the year after.

That is a drop from 37 percent to 13 percent among the people we reached, and we are proud of it. We also want to be plain about its limits:

- **It is self-reported.** People may forget a fall, or not want to admit one.
- **There is no comparison group.** We cannot say how many of these homeowners would have fallen less anyway.
- **The 27 people we did not reach may be different.** Of those 27, we know that 3 died, 2 moved into care facilities and 1 moved in with family. Some of the others may have been harder to reach because their health had declined.

So we can say that, among the homeowners we reached, far fewer reported a fall after repairs than before. We cannot say that our repairs prevented a specific number of falls. In FY2027 we plan to ask the county emergency medical service whether it can share de-identified fall-call counts by address, which would give us a check that does not depend on memory.

## FY2027 plan: a second crew

The waitlist is the problem. At current capacity, a homeowner who applies today waits five and a half months, and some of them will not be able to stay in their home that long.

We plan to add a second crew in FY2027 so we can serve **120 homes**, up from 84, and bring the average wait down to **under three months** by June 30, 2027.

| Second-crew cost item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Second crew lead and one tradesperson (wages and benefits) | $118,000 |
| Second work truck, trailer and tools | $27,000 |
| Materials and trades for 36 additional homes | $85,000 |
| **Total second-crew cost** | **$230,000** |

That brings the full FY2027 budget to $642,000 ($412,000 for current operations plus $230,000 for the second crew).

**How we plan to pay for the second crew:**

- Ostrander County has committed an additional $60,000 for FY2027, contingent on the board of commissioners' budget vote.
- We will raise $55,000 through a spring donor campaign, building on the $78,500 individual donors gave this year.
- We are seeking **$115,000**, half of the second-crew cost, from a single lead funder.

Without the lead gift, we will hire the tradesperson only, serve about 96 homes, and the waitlist will keep growing.
