# Copperleaf Mobile Dental: 2025-26 School Year Report (Sample)

*Sample organization. Copperleaf Mobile Dental, Calder County, and every person named here are fictional.*

School year August 2025 to June 2026. Prepared by the program staff for schools, partners and the board.

## Who we are

Copperleaf Mobile Dental is a dental clinic on wheels. A 38-foot van with two dental chairs, an X-ray unit and its own
generator parks outside a school, and children walk out of class to see us with a signed form from home. We clean
teeth, place sealants, paint on fluoride varnish, fill cavities and treat infections. Nothing we do costs a family
money: we bill Medicaid or the state children's health plan when a child is covered, and grants cover the rest.

We started in 2014 with one van and six schools. Calder County is mostly rural. The nearest dentist who takes Medicaid
for children is in Bellhaven, a 70-minute drive each way from the county's south end, and the wait for a first visit
there is about four months.

Our staff is one dentist three days a week, two dental hygienists, one dental assistant who also drives the van, and a
program manager. Twenty-two volunteers from the county's two high-school health classes help with sign-in and forms.

## The year in numbers

- **Children seen.** 2,684 children at 31 elementary schools, up from 2,210 children at 27 schools the year before.
- **Sealants.** 7,915 sealants placed on the back teeth of 1,903 children. A sealant costs us about $14 to place and
  prevents most cavities on that tooth for years.
- **Fluoride varnish.** 2,540 children.
- **Urgent care.** 412 children were in pain or had an infection when we first saw them. We treated 338 of them on the
  van. The other 74 needed care we cannot give on the van, such as a root canal or oral surgery, and we drove 51 of them
  with a parent to a clinic in Bellhaven. The remaining 23 families chose their own dentist or did not follow up.
- **Days on the road.** The van saw children on 148 school days. It was out of service for 19 school days, mostly for
  the generator and the air conditioning. Each lost day cancels about 18 visits.

## Is it working

Every two years we screen the same twelve schools again before treating anyone, using the state's screening form. In
the fall of 2023, 34% of the children screened at those schools had a cavity that had never been treated. In the
fall of 2025 it was 21%. Over the same two years, the share of children at those schools with at least one sealant
rose from 29% to 58%.

We cannot say all of that change came from the van. Two of the twelve schools also began a toothbrushing program in
2024. But the schools where we have worked longest show the largest drops.

## What teachers and nurses told us

In April we asked every teacher at our 31 schools three questions, and 214 answered.

- 81% said at least one child in their class missed school or could not focus because of tooth pain this year.
- 64% said they had sent a child to the office for tooth pain at least once.
- Asked what would help most, the most common answer was "come more often" (49%), well ahead of "see more children
  each visit" (22%).

A school nurse at Pell Ridge Elementary wrote: "Before the van, I handed out ice and called home. Now I hand out the
van's sign-up form, and the same child comes back to class that afternoon."

## The waiting schools

Nine more elementary schools in Calder County have asked to join. Together they enroll about 1,150 children; that count
comes from the two school districts, not from us. Most of the nine are at the county's south end, near the Bellhaven
road, where families drive farthest for care.

We cannot add them with one van. Our 31 schools already fill every day the van can run, and each school gets two visits
a year, one in the fall and one in the spring. When the van is out of service, a school's visit is pushed, and some
children wait a full extra season.

## The van

The van was built in 2014 and has 214,000 miles on it. Our mechanic says the engine and frame are sound for about five
more years with care, but the generator and air conditioning are near the end of their lives. Repairs cost us $31,400
this year, up from $12,900 the year before.

## Money

Income in 2025-26 was $968,000 and costs were $962,000, a small surplus of $6,000.

Medicaid and the state plan paid for a little more than half of the year. The county contract is renewed every two
years and runs through June 2027.

## What a visit costs

Dividing the year's costs by the children we saw, each child cost us about $358 for the year, usually two visits.

## Looking ahead

To reach the nine waiting schools, we need a second, smaller van and a second team to run it. A 24-foot van with one
chair can park at a small school and cover the south end without the long drive from our base in Copper Falls.

Staff priced it with two vendors. The lower bid for the van, fitted out with a chair, X-ray and generator, is $286,000,
a one-time cost. The second team, one hygienist and one assistant who drives, would cost $148,000 a year, with the
dentist's added days included.

Once the second van is running, Medicaid and the state plan should pay for about half of the new team's cost each year,
based on the share of children covered at the nine schools. That is our estimate from the districts' numbers, and we
will report what the billing really shows. The second van would also let us keep the fall and spring visits on time
when the first van is in the shop.
