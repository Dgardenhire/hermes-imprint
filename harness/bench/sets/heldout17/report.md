# Tallow Creek Recovery Commons
## FY2026 Annual Program Report
### July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026

Tallow Creek Recovery Commons is a peer-run recovery support organization in Ostrander Falls, a river town of about 31,000 people and the seat of Merrow County. Everyone who works directly with participants here has lived experience of addiction, mental illness, or both, and holds or is working toward state peer recovery specialist certification. We do not provide clinical treatment. We walk alongside people before, during, and after it.

This report covers our fourth full fiscal year. It was the first year we ran all four programs at full staffing for twelve months.

## What we ran

**Peer Recovery Coaching.** One-to-one coaching with a certified peer specialist, weekly or more often, for as long as a person wants it. Coaches help with treatment navigation, housing and benefits paperwork, court and probation requirements, and the plain daily work of staying in recovery.

**The Commons Drop-In Center.** Our storefront on Lower Ferry Street is open six days a week, 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. It offers coffee, a phone and computer bank, mutual-aid meetings hosted by outside groups, and a staffed front desk where anyone can ask for a coach.

**The Warm Line.** A non-emergency peer support phone line. Callers reach a trained peer who listens, problem-solves, and connects them to coaching or services. The line operates 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily. Calls outside those hours go to a recorded message that lists the 988 crisis line.

**Family Circle.** A biweekly support group and a six-session skills series for parents, partners, and adult children of people in recovery.

## FY2026 by the numbers

| Measure | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| People enrolled in peer coaching | 318 | 412 |
| Coaching sessions delivered | 2,140 | 2,860 |
| Drop-in center visits | 5,020 | 6,140 |
| Unique drop-in visitors | 961 | 1,188 |
| Warm line calls answered | 1,410 | 1,935 |
| After-hours calls sent to recording | not tracked | 1,112 |
| Families in Family Circle | 51 | 74 |
| Peer specialists on staff (FTE) | 6.5 | 8.0 |

Two numbers on that table shaped our planning for next year. The first is 1,935 warm line calls answered. The second is the 1,112 calls that came in between 10 p.m. and 8 a.m. and reached only a recording. We began counting after-hours calls in August 2025, when our new phone system made it possible. Those calls were 37 percent of all calls the line received. Our staff did not expect the share to be that high.

## The result we are proudest of, and its limit

Of the 412 people who enrolled in peer coaching, 68 percent were still actively meeting with a coach 90 days after their first session. In FY2025, the 90-day figure was 54 percent. We attribute much of the increase to two changes: assigning every new participant a named coach within 48 hours instead of placing them on a waitlist, and offering transportation help (bus passes and rides) to coaching appointments.

We want to be careful about what this number means. It measures continued engagement with coaching, not sobriety, not reduced overdoses, and not improved health. It is also our own count. We have no comparison group, and people who choose to stay in coaching may differ from those who leave. About one in five participants who stopped coming could not be reached for an exit conversation, so we do not know why they left. We plan to bring in an outside evaluator next year so this measure is checked by someone other than us.

## In a participant's words

"I called at two in the morning once and got a recording. I called at two in the afternoon and got Dee, who'd been exactly where I was. I'm still here because of the afternoon call." (R., coaching participant, shared with permission)

We closed the year with a surplus of $8,400, which went to our operating reserve. The reserve now stands at about seven weeks of expenses. The county contract and the settlement subgrant are both restricted to existing programs, so neither can pay for new hours on the warm line.

## FY2027 plan: an overnight warm line

Our main goal for FY2027 is to staff the warm line overnight, 10 p.m. to 8 a.m., seven nights a week, so that callers in those hours reach a person instead of a recording. Overnight callers will still be told about 988 and handed off to it in any emergency. The warm line remains a non-crisis service.

The one-year cost of the overnight expansion is $95,000.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Two part-time overnight peer specialists (wages and benefits) | $58,500 |
| Clinical supervision and on-call backup contract | $12,000 |
| Training and certification for overnight staff | $7,200 |
| Phone system upgrade and call-tracking software | $6,800 |
| Outside evaluation of warm line and coaching outcomes | $6,500 |
| Administrative share | $4,000 |
| **Total** | **$95,000** |

Merrow County Behavioral Health Services has agreed in writing to amend our contract by $35,000 for FY2027 to cover part of this cost, provided we secure the rest. We are seeking the remaining $60,000 from a private funder.

We will judge the overnight line's first year by three measures: the share of after-hours calls answered by a person (target: 90 percent), the number of overnight callers who go on to enroll in coaching, and caller follow-up feedback gathered by the outside evaluator.
