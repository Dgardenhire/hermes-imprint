# Kettle Lane Youth Theatre
## FY2026 Annual Program Report
### July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026 | Ambry Falls

Kettle Lane Youth Theatre teaches young people ages 9 to 17 to make plays: acting, writing, building sets, running lights, and standing in front of a room without apologizing for being there. We work out of the second floor of the old Granary building on Kettle Lane, plus two school partner sites, and we take a small touring show into classrooms across Ambry County.

This report covers our fiscal year 2026. It is written for our board, our families, and the funders who carried us through it.

## The year in numbers

- **142 students** enrolled in the after-school company across three sites: the Granary (78), Brindle Elementary (36), and Ashgrove Middle School (28).
- **61 percent** of after-school students (87 of 142) received full or partial tuition aid. No student was turned away for cost.
- **48 students** attended the four-week summer intensive, up from 39 in FY2025.
- **3 mainstage productions** with **11 public performances** and **2,410 tickets** sold or given away.
- **31 touring performances** of our school-day show, *The Weathervane*, in **14 schools**, reaching **6,820 students** in grades 3 through 8.
- **83 percent** of eligible students returned from FY2025 (98 of 118 who had not aged out).
- **5 staff** (3 full-time, 2 part-time) and **14 teaching artists** on contract.

## Programs

### After-school company

The core of the work. Students meet twice a week for 30 weeks, in mixed-age ensembles of 12 to 16. Each ensemble builds one short original piece in the fall and joins a mainstage production in the spring. Older students rotate through crew roles, and every student spends at least one production backstage. We believe a kid who has run a light board understands a play differently from one who has only stood in it.

This year the Brindle Elementary site moved from one day a week to two, at the school's request. Ashgrove Middle School was new in FY2026 and filled its 28 seats by the second week of October.

### Mainstage productions

- *The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter* (November): an adaptation written with our 13-to-17 ensemble. 4 performances, 862 tickets.
- *Twelfth Night, Shortened* (March): 70 minutes, cast of 22. 4 performances, 931 tickets.
- *Small Town, Big Weather* (May): devised by students from interviews with Ambry Falls residents about the 1998 flood. 3 performances, 617 tickets.

About one ticket in five was given free to families through the Ambry Falls Public Library pass program.

### Touring: *The Weathervane*

A 40-minute, three-actor show about a girl who stops speaking after her family moves, followed by a 20-minute workshop led by the actors. We performed it 31 times this year in 14 schools. Nine of those schools are in Ambry Falls proper. Five are in the eastern half of the county, where the drive runs up to 70 minutes each way and where, according to the county's own 2025 survey, four of the five schools have no arts teacher on staff beyond music.

Demand outran us. We turned down 11 performance requests from eastern county schools because we had one van and one touring cast.

### Summer intensive

Four weeks, five days a week, 48 students, ending in a showcase of scenes and original monologues. Tuition was $640, with aid available on the same scale as the after-school program.

## What we learned

We asked after-school students to answer the same short survey in September and again in May. One question asked whether they agreed with the statement "I feel confident speaking in front of my class at school."

**In September, 41 percent agreed. In May, 74 percent agreed.**

We are proud of that number, and we want to be plain about its limits. It is self-reported. We have no comparison group, so we cannot say how much of the change came from us and how much came from a year of growing up. And only 97 of 142 students completed both surveys; the students who left mid-year or missed survey days may not look like the ones who stayed. In FY2027 we plan to work with an outside evaluator, add a short teacher rating at two partner schools, and track students who do not finish the year.

What we can say with more confidence is that students keep coming back. 83 percent of eligible students re-enrolled, and the waitlist for the 13-to-17 ensemble reached 19 names in August.

One of those students, Wren, age 13, said this at the end-of-year circle in May:

> "I used to eat lunch in the library so nobody would talk to me. Now I'm the one who shows the new kids where the stage door is."

## Finances

Total FY2026 expenses were **$412,600**. Total revenue was **$416,200**, leaving a small operating surplus of **$3,600**, which goes to our cash reserve (now equal to about seven weeks of operating costs).

Program costs (staff, production, touring, and the share of rent used for classes) came to about 81 percent of spending.

### Funding mix

| Source | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Tuition and ticket sales | $131,200 | 32% |
| Individual donors and spring benefit (312 donors) | $118,400 | 28% |
| Halvorsen Family Foundation, general operating | $80,000 | 19% |

| School touring fees (14 schools) | $41,600 | 10% |

Tuition covers less than a third of what we spend, by design. Our aid policy means families pay what they can, and the gap is closed by donors and grants. The Halvorsen grant is in year two of a three-year commitment.

## FY2027 plan: an Eastern County touring company

Our biggest unmet need is the eastern half of the county. In FY2027 we will add a second touring company dedicated to those schools, so that *The Weathervane* and a new companion show for grades 6 through 8 can reach schools we currently turn away.

**Goal:** 20 additional school visits in eastern Ambry County, reaching an estimated 3,400 more students, with a follow-up workshop at each school.

**Cost: $68,500**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Two touring teaching artists (school year) | $44,000 |
| Second van lease and fuel | $9,600 |
| Production for the new companion show | $8,900 |
| Outside evaluation partner | $6,000 |
| **Total** | **$68,500** |

**Committed or projected so far: $23,500.** That is $12,000 in touring fees from the new schools (charged on a sliding scale, $600 per visit on average) and $11,500 from the Ambry County Arts Council's rural access fund, approved in September.

**Remaining gap: $45,000.** We are seeking that amount from a single funder so the company can start in January 2027, at the beginning of the spring semester.
