# Thistledown Rides: FY2026 Annual Report (Sample)

*Sample organization. Thistledown Rides, Marrow County, and every person named here are fictional.*

Fiscal year July 2025 to June 2026. Prepared by staff for members, riders, drivers and the board.

## Who we are

Thistledown Rides gives older adults in Marrow County free rides to medical appointments. A rider calls our office at
least two days ahead, a trained volunteer driver picks them up at home in their own car, waits during the visit, and
brings them home. Riders must be 65 or older or have a disability that keeps them from driving. Most of our riders
live alone. The county has no bus service outside the town of Kestrel, and a taxi from the hill towns to the regional
hospital in Kestrel costs about $90 each way.

We began in 2017 with eight drivers from two churches. Today our staff is a director, Odalys Venn-Carraway, and a
part-time scheduler, Hollis Aberkrombie, who answers the ride line on weekdays from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.

## The year in numbers

- **Rides.** We gave 4,312 one-way rides, up from 3,240 the year before.
- **Riders.** 486 different riders used the service, up from 401.
- **Drivers.** 71 active volunteer drivers gave 9,640 hours and drove about 118,000 miles.
- **Appointments.** 61 percent of rides were to primary care or specialist visits, 22 percent to dialysis or cancer
  treatment, and the rest to physical therapy, eye care and the pharmacy.
- **Turned down.** We could not fill 538 ride requests, about one in nine. Most were requests made less than two days
  ahead, or for early-morning dialysis, when few drivers are free.

## What riders told us

In our April 2026 rider survey (212 riders answered), 84 percent said they would have missed at least one appointment
in the past year without Thistledown. 39 percent said they had put off care because they could not get a ride before
they found us. One rider from Pell Hollow wrote: "My daughter lives four hours away. Before you, I rescheduled my eye
surgery twice."

## Dialysis riders

Dialysis is the hardest need to meet. A dialysis patient needs three rides a week, every week, usually starting before
7 a.m. We drove 31 dialysis riders this year, and 19 of the 538 rides we could not fill were dialysis rides. When a
dialysis ride falls through, the patient either pays for a taxi or misses a treatment. The regional hospital's kidney
unit told us that two of our riders were hospitalized after missed treatments this year. We do not know whether a ride
was the cause.

## Drivers

Our drivers are mostly retired, and 23 of the 71 are over 75 themselves. Fourteen drivers stopped this year, most
for their own health. We trained 22 new drivers. Every driver passes a background check and a driving-record check
and takes a two-hour safety course. Drivers are paid back 45 cents a mile for gas if they ask; about half do.

## Money

FY2026 spending was $164,800: staff $98,500, mileage paid back to drivers $26,600, insurance $18,900, background checks
and training $7,200, phone and scheduling software $6,400, and office costs $7,200.

Income was $171,200: the county's Area Agency on Aging grant $72,000, two foundation grants $48,000, church and
individual gifts $38,700, and a United Way grant of $12,500. Riders pay nothing. Each one-way ride cost us about
$38.

## Looking ahead

The ride line is open only four hours a day, and Hollis is also the person who matches drivers to rides. Staff
estimate that a full-time ride coordinator could fill about 300 more requests a year, mostly by recruiting and
scheduling a standing team of dialysis drivers and by keeping the line open until 5 p.m. The board has asked staff
to find the funding before the county's grant cycle in spring.
