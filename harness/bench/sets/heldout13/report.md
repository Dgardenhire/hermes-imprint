# Quillmere Rolling Library
## FY2026 Annual Program Report (July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026)

Quillmere Rolling Library is a two-person bookmobile nonprofit based in Vashti Bend. We carry a lending collection to rural elementary schools in Corrow County that have no school librarian and sit more than 25 miles from the nearest public library branch. This report covers our sixth fiscal year.

### What we did

During the 2025-26 school year we served 14 elementary schools with a combined K-6 enrollment of 1,180 students. Each school got a visit every other week for 36 weeks, which put 252 school stops on the calendar. We also ran 40 summer stops at churches, a feed store parking lot, and the Corrow County fairgrounds, for a planned total of 292 stops.

We made 281 of those 292 stops. The 11 we missed were all caused by our vehicle, a 2009 bookmobile that crossed 214,000 miles this spring. It lost a transmission line in October, failed its brake inspection in February, and overheated twice in June. Each missed school stop means a class goes four weeks between visits, not two, and the teachers notice.

Key numbers for FY2026:

- Stops completed: 281 of 292 scheduled (96 percent)
- Items checked out: 21,460, up from 18,930 in FY2025
- Return rate: 91 percent
- New county library cards issued to students: 846
- Collection size at year end: 6,820 items
- Summer reading enrollment: 214 children; 131 finished the eight-book goal
- Teachers using our classroom crate loans: 52 of 61

The crate loans were new this year. A teacher picks a theme, such as weather or county history or chapter books for a reluctant fourth grade, and we leave a crate of 30 books in the classroom between visits. Fifty-two teachers used at least one crate. That accounts for about 4,100 of the year's checkouts, so a good share of our growth comes from this one change and not from more students walking up the steps.

### One result, and what it does and does not show

Six of our 14 schools agreed to share their spring reading benchmark results for third grade. At those six schools, the share of third graders reading at or above grade level rose from 41 percent in spring 2025 to 49 percent in spring 2026. That covers 188 students.

We want to be plain about the limits. Six schools is not all 14, and the six that said yes may be the schools with the most engaged principals. We have no comparison group. And in the same year, the state funded a reading tutoring program that placed part-time tutors in four of those six schools. We cannot separate our effect from the tutors' effect, and we will not claim the eight-point gain as ours. What we can say is that the schools where children read the most books from our shelves were also the schools where reading scores went up. Next year we will ask all 14 schools for data and track checkouts per student so we can look at this more carefully.

### What a teacher told us

Dorrie Akin teaches third grade at Fallow Creek Elementary, one of our longest-serving stops. In May she wrote to us: "Bookmobile Tuesday is the only day the whole class runs to the window. When you missed in February, three kids asked me if you were coming back."

Vehicle costs rose from $19,700 in FY2025 to $31,900 this year, almost all of it repairs. That jump is the clearest sign that the current vehicle is near the end of its working life.

### FY2027 plan: Route Eighteen

Four more schools in the north end of the county, Dunmore, Pike Hollow, Sayer Flat and East Kettle, have asked to join. Together they enroll 310 K-6 students. None has a school librarian. We cannot add them with one aging vehicle that already misses stops.

Our plan for FY2027 is called Route Eighteen. We will buy a smaller second vehicle, a used high-roof cargo van refitted with shelving and a wheelchair lift, to run the north route. The 2009 bookmobile will stay in service on the shorter southern route through the end of FY2028 while we save for its replacement. Two vehicles also mean that a breakdown on one route no longer cancels every stop.

If Route Eighteen is fully funded by March 2027, the van will be bought and refitted in spring, run summer stops on the north route in June 2027, and start full school service in August 2027. At that point we expect to serve 18 schools and about 1,490 students with 324 school stops a year.

### Looking ahead

Our goals for FY2027 are simple: miss no more than three stops, get benchmark data from all 14 current schools, and have the Route Eighteen van running north-county summer stops by June 2027.

Marisol Quennell, Executive Director
Quillmere Rolling Library, Vashti Bend
