# Cogline Bike Works
## FY2026 Annual Program Report
### July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026
Pellam Ridge

## Who we are

Cogline Bike Works is a nonprofit repair shop and apprenticeship program on Granger Street in Pellam Ridge. We take in donated and abandoned bicycles, and adults who are out of work or underemployed learn to fix them under a paid, 16-week apprenticeship. Apprentices earn a stipend while they train. The bikes they rebuild are either sold through our shop at low prices or given free to residents who need transportation to work or school.

We have three staff: an executive director, a lead instructor, and a half-time shop manager. Our training follows the entry-level skills used by independent bike shops and regional repair employers: wheel truing, brake and drivetrain service, hub overhaul, frame inspection, customer intake, and basic shop math.

## FY2026 at a glance

| Measure | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apprentices enrolled | 18 | 26 | +44% |
| Apprentices who completed 16 weeks | 12 | 19 | +58% |
| Completion rate | 67% | 73% | +6 points |
| Graduates employed within 90 days | 8 of 12 | 14 of 19 | +6 people |
| 90-day employment rate | 67% | 74% | +7 points |
| Bikes refurbished | 310 | 482 | +55% |
| Bikes given free to residents | 140 | 205 | +46% |
| Shop earned income | $41,200 | $58,600 | +42% |

The year's growth came mostly from one change. In September 2025 we moved from one cohort at a time to two overlapping cohorts, starting eight weeks apart. That let the lead instructor keep a full bench every week. It also let second-half apprentices coach first-half apprentices on basic jobs, which is close to how a real shop works.

## Program results

### Enrollment and completion

We enrolled 26 apprentices in FY2026, up from 18. Nineteen finished the full 16 weeks. Of the seven who did not finish, four left for other jobs before graduating, two left for family or health reasons, and one was dismissed for repeated absences. We count all seven as non-completers, including the four who left for work.

### Employment

Fourteen of 19 graduates were working within 90 days of finishing. Nine were hired by bike shops or bike rental and fleet operators in the county. Three took jobs in related mechanical trades: one small-engine shop, one appliance repair company, and one forklift maintenance crew at a distribution center. Two were hired by Cogline itself as part-time shop assistants. Median starting wage among the 14 was $17.25 an hour, compared with $13.10 reported at intake.

**An honest caveat on this number.** The 74% figure is a 90-day measure, and the group is small. A single graduate changes the rate by about five points. Seven of the 14 placements were confirmed by an employer. The other seven come from the graduate's own report by phone or text. We do not yet have one-year retention for the FY2026 group. For the FY2025 group, we reached 11 of 12 graduates at the one-year mark: 7 of the 8 who had been employed at 90 days were still working, and one of the four who had not found work at 90 days had since been hired. That is encouraging, but it is one cohort of twelve people, and we would not call it a trend yet. Starting in FY2027 we will ask every graduate for employer contact consent at graduation so more placements can be confirmed directly.

### Bikes and the community

Apprentices rebuilt 482 bikes this year. We sold 277 through the shop, mostly between $60 and $180, and gave 205 free to residents referred by the Pellam Ridge adult education center, two family shelters, and the county reentry office. Every free bike leaves with a lock, lights, and a fitted helmet.

## A participant's view

Dariel M. graduated in the spring cohort and now works the service desk at a shop in the county seat.

> "I came in thinking I'd learn to fix flats. What I actually learned was how to look at a problem, write down what's wrong, and give somebody a straight price. That's the part they hired me for."

## Expenses

Total FY2026 operating expenses were $312,400.

| Category | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Apprentice stipends | $118,800 | 38% |
| Staff salaries and benefits (2.5 FTE) | $121,500 | 39% |
| Rent and utilities | $28,800 | 9% |
| Parts, tools, and safety gear | $24,600 | 8% |
| Insurance, accounting, and administration | $18,700 | 6% |
| **Total** | **$312,400** | **100%** |

Stipends and staff together are 77% of spending. Stipends rose from $82,100 in FY2025 because we enrolled eight more apprentices. Cost per enrolled apprentice fell from $13,900 in FY2025 to $12,015 in FY2026, because rent, insurance, and management costs were spread across more people.

## Funding mix

Total FY2026 revenue was $318,000, leaving a surplus of $5,600 that went to our operating reserve.

| Source | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Tallis County Workforce Board training contract | $120,000 | 38% |
| Ferncliff Community Foundation general operating grant | $75,000 | 24% |
| Shop earned income (bike sales and repairs) | $58,600 | 18% |
| Individual donors and the Spring Gear-Up Ride | $44,400 | 14% |
| Northway Cycle Supply (cash and parts sponsorship) | $20,000 | 6% |
| **Total** | **$318,000** | **100%** |

The Workforce Board contract is our largest single source and pays a set amount per enrolled apprentice. The contract runs through June 2027. Earned income grew for the second straight year and now covers more than our rent, utilities, and parts combined.

## FY2027 plan: the evening cohort

Most of the people on our waitlist already work a daytime job, often part-time or on shifting schedules, and cannot leave it for a weekday program. At the end of FY2026 our waitlist stood at 31 people, and 22 of them said they could only attend evenings.

In FY2027 we will add an evening cohort that meets Monday through Thursday, 5:30 to 9:00 p.m., for 20 weeks to cover the same hours as the day program. This raises total enrollment from 26 to 36 apprentices.

| Evening cohort cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Second instructor (1.0 FTE, salary and benefits) | $52,000 |
| Stipends for 10 evening apprentices | $30,000 |
| Added tools, workstands, and safety gear | $8,500 |
| Evening utilities and building access | $5,500 |
| **Total** | **$96,000** |

The Tallis County Workforce Board has committed $36,000 toward the evening cohort under its per-apprentice rate. That leaves $60,000 we must raise from a new funder before the cohort can start in February 2027. Our full FY2027 budget, with the evening cohort, is $408,400.

Our targets for FY2027 are 36 apprentices enrolled, at least 26 completing, at least 70% of completers employed within 90 days, and at least half of all placements confirmed directly with an employer.
