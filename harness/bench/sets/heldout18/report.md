# Haverlin Creek Paddle Works
## FY2026 Annual Program Report (July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026)

Haverlin Creek Paddle Works is a small youth canoe and kayak program on the lower reach of Haverlin Creek in Ostrander Gap. We teach children ages 9 to 17 to paddle, rescue themselves and others, and read moving water. Every participant pays on a sliding scale, and no one is turned away for cost. We run from a leased boathouse at Tansey Ford Landing with two year-round staff, five seasonal instructors and about 40 volunteers.

This report covers our fourth full season.

## What we did this year

FY2026 was our largest season. We ran four program tracks:

- **First Strokes**, a six-week introductory course for new paddlers, ages 9 to 12, held after school in spring and early fall.
- **River Skills**, a summer day program for returning paddlers, ages 11 to 15, covering eddy turns, ferrying, wet exits and assisted rescues.
- **Creek Crew**, a teen leadership track, ages 15 to 17, where older paddlers assist instructors and earn an entry-level paddling instructor certificate.
- **Open Water Saturdays**, free drop-in sessions for families, held twice a month from May through September.

## Two-year trend

| Measure | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Youth enrolled (unduplicated) | 142 | 188 | 231 |
| On-water program hours delivered | 1,860 | 2,540 | 3,310 |
| Youth on full or partial fee waiver | 61 (43%) | 87 (46%) | 118 (51%) |
| Returning paddlers from prior year | 38 | 69 | 94 |
| Creek Crew teens certified | 4 | 7 | 11 |
| Family drop-in visits | 310 | 455 | 612 |

Enrollment grew 23 percent from FY2025 to FY2026, and 63 percent over two years. On-water hours grew faster than enrollment because more paddlers returned for a second or third season and stayed in longer programs. Just over half of this year's paddlers received a fee waiver, up from 43 percent two years ago. That shift is deliberate: in FY2025 we began recruiting through the Ostrander Gap Unified School District's free-lunch program and two after-school sites on the east side of town.

## Results

**Water safety skills.** Every First Strokes participant takes a skills assessment at the start and end of the course: a 50-yard swim in a life jacket, a wet exit from a capsized kayak, and a self-rescue back into the boat with help. In FY2026, 84 percent of First Strokes paddlers who took the end-of-course assessment passed all three skills, up from 71 percent in FY2025.

We want to be honest about what that number does and does not show. Only 162 of the 197 First Strokes paddlers who started the course took the final assessment. The other 35 missed the last session, most often because of transportation problems or a schedule conflict, and we do not know how they would have scored. We also changed assessors partway through the year when our lead instructor went on leave, and the two assessors did not score in exactly the same way. The improvement is real enough that we believe the new two-instructor-per-boat-group model is working, but we cannot yet say how much of the 13-point gain comes from better teaching and how much comes from who stayed until the end. Next year we will run the final assessment during the second-to-last session and use a single written scoring sheet for every assessor.

**Retention.** 94 paddlers returned from FY2025, which is 50 percent of last year's enrollment. Returning paddlers are the backbone of River Skills and Creek Crew.

**Leadership.** Eleven Creek Crew teens earned their entry-level instructor certificate. Six of them worked as paid junior instructors during summer 2026, at $15 an hour.

## Next year: the Weekday Water program

Our waitlist is the clearest sign of unmet need. In spring 2026, 74 children were on the First Strokes waitlist, and most of them were middle schoolers from the east-side after-school sites who could only attend on weekdays. Our current schedule cannot absorb them, and we have no boats designed for paddlers with physical disabilities, which has meant saying no to four families this year.

In FY2027 we plan to launch **Weekday Water**, a year-round weekday program for middle school paddlers, ages 11 to 14, with adaptive equipment. The program will serve an estimated 60 additional youth in its first year.

| Weekday Water cost item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Seasonal program coordinator (9 months) | $34,000 |
| Six recreational kayaks and two adaptive outrigger canoes | $16,500 |
| Twelve-month lease on a 12-passenger van | $7,500 |
| **Total new cost** | **$58,000** |

We have asked the Tansey County Recreation District to increase its grant by $18,000 to cover part of this cost, and the district has signaled support pending its January budget vote. We are seeking the remaining **$40,000** from a new foundation partner.
