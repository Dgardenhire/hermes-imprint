# Vessick Neighborhood Mediation Center
## FY2026 Annual Program Report
### July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026

Vessick Neighborhood Mediation Center (VNMC) helps people in Vessick and the surrounding river townships settle disputes before they reach a courtroom, a police call, or a moving truck. We are a small organization: two and a half paid staff, a rented office above the Coldwater Street pharmacy, two mediation rooms, and a roster of trained volunteer mediators who do most of the work. This report covers what we did in FY2026, what it cost, who paid for it, and what we plan to build next.

## The year in numbers

| Measure | FY2026 |
|---|---|
| Referrals received | 412 |
| Cases opened | 296 |
| Cases that reached a mediation session | 231 |
| Written agreements signed | 168 |
| Agreement rate (of cases mediated) | 73% |
| Active volunteer mediators | 38 |
| New mediators certified | 11 |
| Volunteer hours logged | 2,140 |
| Sessions held with an interpreter | 54 |

Referrals came from the Vessick County District Court (41%), the city's code enforcement office and police non-emergency line (22%), schools (9%), and people who called us directly or were sent by a neighbor (28%). Not every referral becomes a case. About a quarter of the time, one side declines to take part, or the problem resolves on its own once someone neutral picks up the phone.

## Programs

**Neighbor and community disputes.** Fences, noise, shared driveways, dogs, trees on the property line, a parking spot that two households both believe is theirs. These made up 121 of our 296 cases. They are rarely about the fence. A typical case takes two sessions of about 90 minutes each, and most end with a written agreement both parties sign before they leave the room.

**Landlord-tenant early resolution.** Our fastest-growing program. In FY2026 we opened 118 landlord-tenant cases, up from 74 the year before. Most involved back rent, security deposits, repairs, or a notice to vacate. Of those 118 cases, 87 reached a mediation session and 61 ended in a signed agreement, usually a payment plan, a repair schedule, or an agreed move-out date that gave a family time to find a new place.

**School peer mediation.** We trained 46 students at Lindell Middle School and Harrow Road Middle School to mediate conflicts between classmates. Each school now has a student mediation team that meets weekly with a faculty sponsor. Schools pay a sliding-scale fee for the training.

**Volunteer mediator training.** Our 40-hour basic mediation course certified 11 new volunteers this year, and our active roster stands at 38. Volunteers include a retired postal carrier, two nursing students, a former city council aide, and a bilingual bookkeeper who now co-mediates most of our Spanish-language sessions. Together they gave 2,140 hours.

## What changed for people

The clearest result this year came from the landlord-tenant program. Ninety days after each landlord-tenant agreement was signed, our case coordinator called both parties to ask whether the agreement was still holding. We reached 52 of the 61 households. In 44 of those 52, the agreement was holding: the payment plan was current, the repair was done, or the move happened on the agreed date. That is 85%.

We want to be careful with that number. It is a self-reported answer to a phone call, not a review of court records. We could not reach 9 of the 61 households, and people who are hard to reach may also be the ones whose agreements fell apart, so the true rate could be lower. We also have no comparison group: we do not know how many of these cases would have settled without us. What we can say is that 44 households told us the deal they made in our office was still working three months later.

A tenant who came to us in March 2026 put it this way:

> "I came in ready to fight about the deposit. I left with a payment plan and my landlord's phone number. Nobody had to lose."

Total FY2026 expenses were **$286,400**.

| Category | Amount |
|---|---|
| Personnel (1.0 executive director, 1.0 case coordinator, 0.5 training coordinator) | $198,600 |
| Rent and utilities (office and two mediation rooms) | $31,800 |
| Insurance, accounting, and case-management software | $17,400 |
| Volunteer training and certification | $14,200 |
| Interpreter services | $12,900 |
| School program materials and student stipends | $11,500 |
| **Total** | **$286,400** |

Personnel is 69% of our budget. That is the work: someone has to answer the phone, schedule two parties who do not want to be in the same room, train the volunteers, and sit in on the hard sessions.

## Funding

Total FY2026 revenue was **$291,750**, from five sources.

| Source | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Vessick County District Court, alternative dispute resolution contract | $112,000 | 38% |
| Halbrook Family Foundation, general operating grant | $75,000 | 26% |
| City of Vessick, housing stability allocation | $48,000 | 16% |
| Individual donors and the October Common Table dinner | $41,250 | 14% |
| Fees for service (school trainings, sliding-scale private mediations) | $15,500 | 5% |

We ended the year with a surplus of $5,350, which went to our operating reserve. The reserve now covers about seven weeks of expenses. Our board's goal is twelve.

The court contract is our largest single source and renews every two years; the current term runs through June 2027. No single funder covers more than 38% of the budget, and we intend to keep it that way.

## FY2027 plan: the Tenant Early Resolution Desk

Most of our landlord-tenant cases reach us late, often after an eviction case has already been filed. By then, positions have hardened, court costs are added to the balance, and a tenant may have a filing on their record even if the case is later dismissed.

Starting in February 2027, we will staff a **Tenant Early Resolution Desk** inside the Vessick County Courthouse on the two mornings each week when the eviction docket is heard. A trained mediator and a volunteer will meet landlords and tenants in the hallway before their hearing, offer a same-day mediation in a side room the court has agreed to provide, and, when that is not possible, schedule a session at our office within five business days.

The first 12 months of the desk will cost **$96,000**:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Housing mediator, 0.6 FTE, salary and benefits | $52,800 |
| Supervision and case coordination (share of existing staff time) | $14,400 |
| Interpreter services on docket days | $9,600 |
| Volunteer mediator stipends and training | $8,400 |
| 90-day and 180-day follow-up calls and outcome tracking | $6,000 |
| Printed materials, laptop, and case-management licenses | $4,800 |
| **Total** | **$96,000** |

The Vessick County District Court has committed **$36,000** toward the desk as an amendment to our existing contract. We are seeking the remaining **$60,000** from one or more foundation partners.

We will measure the desk honestly. Alongside the 90-day follow-up call, we will add a 180-day call, and we have asked the court clerk for an anonymized count of how many desk cases return to the docket within six months. If the numbers do not hold up, we will say so in next year's report.
