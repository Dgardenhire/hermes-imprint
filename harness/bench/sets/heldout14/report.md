# Ridgeline Return Works
## FY2026 Annual Program Report
Fiscal year July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026
Carver Falls

### Who we are

Ridgeline Return Works helps people coming home from prison and county jail get into paid work and keep it. We run out of a converted tire shop on Lowell Street in Carver Falls, with six full-time staff, two part-time crew leads and a board of nine. Most of the people we serve were released to supervision in Tamsen County and have less than ninety days of freedom behind them when they walk through our door.

The model is simple and deliberately short on waiting. A participant starts on a paid transitional work crew within five business days of intake, earning $16.50 an hour on landscaping, light demolition and park cleanup contracts. While they work, a job coach helps them get identification, a resume, a driver's license where possible and interviews with local employers. The goal is a permanent job within twelve weeks, and support for the six months after that.

### What happened this year

FY2026 was the year the waiting list caught up with us. Referrals from Tamsen County Probation and two state facilities rose sharply after the county added a reentry liaison position in the fall, and we expanded the crew schedule from four days a week to five to absorb them.

| Measure | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| People enrolled | 88 | 121 | +38% |
| Completed transitional work phase | 61 | 90 | +48% |
| Placed in permanent jobs | 47 | 69 | +47% |
| Average starting wage at placement | $17.10 | $18.25 | +$1.15 |
| Paid crew hours worked by participants | 14,200 | 19,850 | +40% |
| State IDs or licenses obtained | 52 | 83 | +60% |

Placements went to 23 employers in FY2026, up from 15 the year before. The largest were Brannock Precast, the Carver Falls Public Works Department and three regional warehouse operators. Seven of the 69 placements were in jobs with an apprenticeship track.

### The result we are proudest of, and its limits

Of the people we placed in permanent jobs during FY2026, 71 percent were still employed six months after placement. In FY2025 the same figure was 58 percent.

We want to be careful with that number. Only 52 of the 69 FY2026 placements had reached the six-month mark by the time this report was written; the other 17 were placed in the spring and are not yet counted. Of the 52, we confirmed employment for 37. We lost contact with 6 people, and we count all 6 as not employed, which may understate the real rate. We also cannot say how much of the improvement comes from our work and how much comes from a tighter local labor market, since Tamsen County unemployment fell from 5.1 to 4.4 percent over the same period. We do not have a comparison group, and we are not going to pretend we do.

What we can say is that the people who stayed employed were far more likely to have had at least three check-ins with a job coach in their first sixty days on the job. That pattern drives our plan for next year.

### In their words

Darnell, who finished the crew phase in October and now works the night shift at Brannock Precast, told our staff:

"The crew paid me the first week. That's the part nobody else did. I didn't have to choose between rent and showing up."

### What we learned

Three lessons shaped our planning.

First, the job is not the finish line. The first sixty days after placement are where people lose work, usually over transportation, a scheduling conflict with supervision appointments or a first conflict with a supervisor. Our coaches were stretched across intake, the crew phase and follow-up, and follow-up was what slipped.

Second, a single crew limits who we can take. With one crew of twelve, people referred in busy months waited up to three weeks to start, and we know from our own intake records that people who wait longer are less likely to show up at all.

Third, employers want to keep hiring from us. Four employers asked for more referrals than we could send this year.

### FY2027 plan

In FY2027 we will add a second transitional work crew and a dedicated retention coach whose only job is supporting people in their first six months of permanent work.

| FY2027 expansion item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Second crew lead (full-time) and crew supervisor stipend | $72,000 |
| Retention coach (full-time, including benefits) | $64,000 |
| Second crew vehicle lease and equipment | $28,000 |
| Added participant supports for the larger cohort | $22,000 |
| **Total new cost** | **$186,000** |

With both pieces in place, we expect to enroll 160 people in FY2027, place 90 in permanent jobs and raise six-month retention to 75 percent, measured the same way we measured it this year, with lost contacts counted as not employed.

The full FY2027 budget, including the expansion, is $798,400. We have commitments or strong renewal signals for $612,400 of it from existing sources. The remaining $186,000 is the expansion itself, which we are raising from new and existing funders this winter.
